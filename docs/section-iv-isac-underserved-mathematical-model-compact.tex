\section{ISAC-Assisted Underserved-Region Detection and UAV Repositioning}
\label{sec:mathematical-model}

Let $\mathcal{U}$ and $\mathcal{Q}$ denote the ground UEs and
gNB-mounted UAVs, respectively. Each gNB-mounted UAV carries a co-located
transmit--receive ISAC subsystem and acts as a sensing node; hence,
$\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{Q}$. Terrestrial gNBs and terrestrial sensing
devices do not perform radar sensing. Each ground UE is represented
by a co-located passive target with effective radar cross section
(RCS) $\sigma_u$. The near-RT RIC combines ISAC observations with
communication measurements, predicts future UAV-repositioning hotspots
using a Random Forest (RF), and supplies target centroids to the UAV
repositioning controller.
The prototype represents ISAC at the system/control level through
co-located sensing and communication functions. Joint waveform
design and explicit sensing--communication resource sharing are
outside the present scope.

\subsection{System-Level ISAC Sensing}

Under a monostatic system-level sensing abstraction, the 3-D range
and received echo power between UAV $s$ and UE $u$ are
\begin{align}
 r_{s,u}(t)&=\|\mathbf{p}_s(t)-\mathbf{p}_u(t)\|_2,
 \label{eq:isac-range}\\
 P^{\rm r}_{s,u}(t)&=
 \frac{P_sG_s^2\lambda^2\sigma_u}
 {(4\pi)^3r_{s,u}^4(t)L_s},
 \label{eq:isac-echo}
\end{align}
where $P_s$, $G_s$, $\lambda$, and $L_s$ are the sensing power,
antenna gain, wavelength, and aggregate loss. With noise density
$N_0$, sensing bandwidth $B_s$, and noise factor $F_s$, the echo SNR
is
\begin{equation}
 \gamma_{s,u}(t)=
 \frac{P^{\rm r}_{s,u}(t)}{N_0B_sF_s}.
 \label{eq:isac-snr-compact}
\end{equation}

The logistic function provides a smooth system-level SNR-to-detection
mapping rather than modelling a particular radar detector:
\begin{align}
 p^{\rm D}_{s,u}(t)&=
 \left[1+e^{-a(\gamma^{\rm dB}_{s,u}(t)-\gamma_0)}\right]^{-1},\\
 d_{s,u}(t)&\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}
 \bigl(p^{\rm D}_{s,u}(t)\bigr),
 \label{eq:isac-detection-compact}
\end{align}
where $\gamma^{\rm dB}_{s,u}(t)=10\log_{10}\gamma_{s,u}(t)$,
$a$ is the detection-curve slope, and $\gamma_0$ is the SNR at which
$p^{\rm D}_{s,u}=0.5$. For $d_{s,u}(t)=1$, the horizontal
position observation is
\begin{align}
 \widehat{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_{s,u}(t)
 &=\mathbf{p}^{xy}_u(t)+\mathbf{e}_{s,u}(t),
 \label{eq:isac-position}\\
 \mathbf{e}_{s,u}(t)&\sim\mathcal{N}
 \bigl(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{s,u}^2(t)\mathbf{I}_2\bigr).
\end{align}
This error model is a system-level simulation abstraction. Let
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\gamma_{s,u}(t)=
 \gamma^{\rm dB}_{s,u}(t)-\gamma_{\rm ref}.
\end{equation}
The localisation standard deviation is
\begin{equation}
 \sigma_{s,u}(t)=\operatorname{clip}
 \left(\sigma_{\rm ref}10^{-\Delta\gamma_{s,u}(t)/20},
 \sigma_{\min},\sigma_{\max}\right).
 \label{eq:isac-error-compact}
\end{equation}

Define $\mathcal{S}_u(t)=\{s\in\mathcal{S}:d_{s,u}(t)=1\}$ and
$w_{s,u}(t)=\sigma_{s,u}^{-2}(t)$. Assuming independent, unbiased
Gaussian errors, the RIC forms the inverse-variance estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_u(t)=
 \frac{\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_u(t)}w_{s,u}(t)
 \widehat{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_{s,u}(t)}
 {\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_u(t)}w_{s,u}(t)}.
 \label{eq:isac-fusion-compact}
\end{equation}
No estimate is produced when $\mathcal{S}_u(t)=\varnothing$. Hence,
the detected UE set is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{U}_{\rm det}(t)=
 \{u\in\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{S}_u(t)\neq\varnothing\}.
 \label{eq:detected-set}
\end{equation}
True UE coordinates are used only by the measurement generator and
for offline evaluation and training labels; online control receives
only fused estimates.
Importantly, sensing is attempted for every relevant UE before its
communication service state is evaluated. Thus, UE group identity or
an oracle underserved label is not supplied to the online controller.

\subsection{RF-Based Future Repositioning-Demand Prediction}

Let $A_u(t)=1$ when UE $u$ is normally attached and has a fresh
serving-cell measurement, and let $A_u(t)=0$ otherwise. When
$A_u(t)=1$, its experienced serving RSRP is
\begin{equation}
 R_u^{\rm serv}(t)=R_{u,c_u(t)}(t).
 \label{eq:serving-rsrp}
\end{equation}
The measurable weak-service indicator is
\begin{equation}
 I_u^{\rm weak}(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \mathbf{1}\{R_u^{\rm serv}(t)<R_{\min}\},&A_u(t)=1,\\
 0,&A_u(t)=0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:weak-service}
\end{equation}
The service threshold $R_{\min}$ is intentionally stricter than the
minimum attachment and handover threshold. A UE may therefore remain
connected while still being classified as weak for repositioning
analysis.
Weak serving RSRP alone does not imply a coverage hole because a
handover candidate may already provide adequate service. Let
$\mathcal{C}^{\rm f}_{\rm TN,u}(t)$ and
$\mathcal{C}^{\rm f}_{\rm UAV,u}(t)$ contain candidate TN and UAV
cells, respectively, that satisfy the configured RSRP-freshness and
spare-capacity requirements. Define
\begin{align}
 R_u^{\rm TN}(t)&=
 \max_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm f}_{\rm TN,u}(t)}R_{u,c}(t),\\
 R_u^{\rm UAV}(t)&=
 \max_{c\in\mathcal{C}^{\rm f}_{\rm UAV,u}(t)}R_{u,c}(t),
 \label{eq:best-feasible-rsrp}
\end{align}
where the maximum of an empty set is $-\infty$. Runtime handover
selection prioritises a feasible TN cell and uses a UAV cell only
when TN service is insufficient.

Let $T_u^{\rm unk}(t)$ be the duration of the current unattached or
missing-measurement interval. The service-eligibility gate is
\begin{equation}
 W_u(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 1,&A_u(t)=1,\ R_u^{\rm serv}(t)<R_{\min},\\
 1,&A_u(t)=0,\ T_u^{\rm unk}(t)\geq T_{\rm unk},\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:service-eligibility}
\end{equation}
Thus, a missing radio state cannot be interpreted as weak service
until its UNKNOWN timeout expires. Define the no-alternative gate
\begin{equation}
 N_u(t)=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{
 R_u^{\rm TN}(t)<R_{\min},\quad
 R_u^{\rm UAV}(t)<R_{\min}\right\}.
 \label{eq:no-alternative}
\end{equation}
To avoid repositioning during handover evaluation, the joint weak
or timed-out UNKNOWN and no-alternative condition must persist:
\begin{equation}
\Pi_u(t)=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{
 W_u(\xi)N_u(\xi)=1,\quad
 \forall\xi\in[t-T_{\rm US},t]\right\},
 \label{eq:weak-persistence}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 T_{\rm US}>T_{\rm TTT}+T_{\rm ctrl}.
 \label{eq:persistence-condition}
\end{equation}
Here, $T_{\rm TTT}$ is the handover time-to-trigger and
$T_{\rm ctrl}$ is the UAV-controller evaluation period, which is
distinct from the E2 reporting and logic-module query intervals. The
universal condition is evaluated at the discrete controller epochs within
the stated interval, as in the simulation. The repositioning demand
indicator is therefore
\begin{equation}
 I_u^{\rm move}(t)=\Pi_u(t).
 \label{eq:reposition-trigger}
\end{equation}
For grid cell $g\in\mathcal{G}$, define the sensed assignment
\begin{equation}
 b_{u,g}(t)=
 \mathbf{1}\{\widehat{\mathbf{p}}^{xy}_u(t)\in g\}.
 \label{eq:grid-assignment}
\end{equation}
The detected density, persistent UAV-repositioning demand, and its
share are
\begin{align}
 \rho_g(t)&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rm det}(t)}b_{u,g}(t),\\
 H_g(t)&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}_{\rm det}(t)}
 I_u^{\rm move}(t)b_{u,g}(t),\\
 Z_g(t)&=\frac{H_g(t)}{\max\{\rho_g(t),1\}}.
 \label{eq:grid-statistics}
\end{align}
The exact RF feature vector is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{x}_g(t)=
 [\rho_g,H_g,Z_g,\overline{R}_g,
 \overline{\sigma}_g,\Delta H_g,d_g^{\rm Q}]^{\mathsf T},
 \label{eq:rf-features-compact}
\end{equation}
where $\overline{R}_g$ is the mean serving RSRP over detected UEs in
$g$ having a fresh serving measurement, and $\overline{\sigma}_g$ is
the mean fused localisation uncertainty over all detected UEs in
$g$. Also, $\Delta H_g=H_g(t)-H_g(t-\Delta t)$ and $d_g^{\rm Q}$ is
the distance to the nearest UAV. If no fresh serving-RSRP sample is
available in $g$, set $\overline{R}_g=R_{\rm floor}$. If $\rho_g=0$,
also set $H_g=Z_g=0$ and
$\overline{\sigma}_g=\sigma_{\max}$; $\Delta H_g$ remains the stated
temporal difference.
Here and in the movement equations below, $\Delta t=T_{\rm ctrl}$
denotes the controller update interval. The simulator may use a
smaller internal mobility-integration step. A separately clocked RF
reporter would require its own feature-sampling interval.

Let $\pi_{\rm ref}$ denote a fixed RF-disabled reference policy,
which is the sensed reactive controller in this work. Using the true
future UE positions and the same persistent no-feasible-alternative
criterion, define offline
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 H_{g,\pi_{\rm ref}}^{\rm true}(t+\Delta_p)
 &=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{U}}
 I_{u,\pi_{\rm ref}}^{\rm move}(t+\Delta_p)\\[-1mm]
 &\quad\times
 \mathbf{1}\{\mathbf{p}^{xy}_u(t+\Delta_p)\in g\}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:true-future-hotspot}
\end{equation}
This quantity is generated only from trajectories collected under
$\pi_{\rm ref}$; the proactive RF controller is not active during
label generation. It is used only as a supervised-learning target
and is never exposed to online control. Consequently, the label is
conditional on $\pi_{\rm ref}$ rather than a policy-independent
demand quantity. For prediction horizon
$\Delta_p$, the label is
\begin{equation}
 y_g(t)=\mathbf{1}\!\left\{
 H_{g,\pi_{\rm ref}}^{\rm true}(t+\Delta_p)
 \geq N_{\min}\right\}.
 \label{eq:rf-label-compact}
\end{equation}
The RF estimates
\begin{equation}
 q_g(t)=\Pr\{y_g(t)=1\mid\mathbf{x}_g(t)\},
 \label{eq:rf-probability}
\end{equation}
and selects
\begin{equation}
 \widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm RH}(t)=
 \{g\in\mathcal{G}:q_g(t)\geq\tau\}.
 \label{eq:predicted-hotspots}
\end{equation}
Training, validation, and testing use independent simulation seeds
to prevent temporal leakage. RF hyperparameters and $\Delta_p$ are
reported in the simulation-parameter table.
The RF is the only learned component: it is trained offline, whereas
K-means has no training phase and is re-solved at each online
controller epoch.

\subsection{Predictive Clustering and Repositioning}

Let $\mathcal{Q}_{\rm a}(t)$ contain UAVs with remaining serving
capacity. The number of target clusters is
\begin{equation}
 K(t)=\min\bigl\{|\mathcal{Q}_{\rm a}(t)|,
 |\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm RH}(t)|\bigr\}.
 \label{eq:k-selection}
\end{equation}
If either set is empty, no repositioning action is generated. To
avoid suppressing newly emerging hotspots with the current value of
$H_g(t)$, probability-weighted K-means uses $q_g(t)$ alone:
\begin{equation}
 \{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k^\star\}=
 \arg\min_{\{\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\}}
 \sum_{g\in\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\rm RH}(t)}q_g(t)
 \min_{1\leq k\leq K}
 \|\mathbf{c}_g-\boldsymbol{\mu}_k\|_2^2.
 \label{eq:kmeans-compact}
\end{equation}
Each available UAV is greedily assigned once to its nearest
unassigned centroid, matching the controller. For UAV $j$, define
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\delta}_j(t)=
 \boldsymbol{\mu}_{k(j)}^\star-\mathbf{q}^{xy}_j(t).
\end{equation}
The bounded movement factor is
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_j(t)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\min\!\left\{1,
 \dfrac{v_{\max}\Delta t}{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_j(t)\|_2}\right\},
 &\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_j(t)\|_2>0,\\[1mm]
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:movement-step}
\end{equation}
The horizontal position update is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{q}^{xy}_j(t+\Delta t)=
 \mathbf{q}^{xy}_j(t)+
 \alpha_j(t)\boldsymbol{\delta}_j(t),
 \label{eq:movement-compact}
\end{equation}
while altitude remains fixed and flight-area constraints are
enforced.

We evaluate detection probability, localisation RMSE, matched
hotspot-centroid error, the weak-service and persistent
reposition-trigger fractions, fifth-percentile throughput, and outage
time. The proposed RF-assisted predictive controller is compared
with a static-UAV baseline, an oracle-position reactive K-means
controller, and an ISAC-sensed reactive K-means controller.
